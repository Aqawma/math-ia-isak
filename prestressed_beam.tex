\section{Haybale as a Prestressed Beam}
\subsection{Twine as a Tendon}
To treat the bale as a prestressed section, we must demonstrate that the twine applies a compressive force. Consider a short element of twine subtending an angle $d\alpha$ at the local center of curvature. It carries tension $T$ and is pressed against the hay by a normal load $w_n$ per unit length. The tensions at either end are inclined to each other by $d\alpha$, so the result points inward with magnitude $2T\sin(d\alpha/2)$. As $d\alpha\rightarrow0$, this tends to $Td\alpha$. The element has the length $Rd\alpha$, so balancing forces normal to the twine gives
\[
w_nRd\alpha=Td\alpha\Rightarrow w_n=\frac{T}{R}=T\kappa
\]
Where...
\begin{itemize}
	\item $w_n$ is the force the twine exerts on the hay, per unit length of twine
	\item $T$ is the tension in the twine
	\item $R$ is the local radius of curvature, with $\kappa=\frac{1}{R}$ being the curvature
\end{itemize}

This means the load on the hay is proportional to the curvature. On a flat face, $\kappa=0$, so the twine exerts no force. By imagining a similar shape, like a rubber band, we can intuitively see why this is true. All the load is delivered at the corners of the bale, so it acts like a tendon anchored at the corners. This is why the twine only cuts into the bale at the corners.

\subsubsection{Second Moment of Area}
When a section bends, the warping remains in the plane. For example, a piece of hay at a height $y$ above the neutral axis is stretched only in proportion to $y$, as that is the direction of the bend. Thus, the stress is also proportional to $y$. We can model this stress as a lever arm $y$, so its contribution to the moment carries a second factor of $y$. This leads to the formulae:
\[
\sigma=\frac{My}{I}
\]
Where...
\begin{itemize}
	\item $\sigma$ is the direct stress at a point in the cross section, taken with positive tension ($\text{Nm}^{-2}$).
	\item $M$ is the bending moment carried by that cross section (Nm).
	\item $y$ is the perpendicular distance from the neutral axis to the area element (m).
	\item $I$ is the second moment of area about the neutral axis ($\text{m}^4$).
\end{itemize}
\[
I=\int y^2dA
\]
Where...
\begin{itemize}
	\item $I$ is the second moment of area about the neutral axis ($\text{m}^4$).
	\item $y$ is the perpendicular distance from the neutral axis to the area element (m).
	\item $dA$ is an element of cross-sectional area ($\text{m}^2$)
	\item The integral is taken over the entire cross section.
\end{itemize}
