\section{Background}
\subsection{Making \& Binding of Square Bales}
To understand the mathematics that appears later in the paper, one must first understand three things. First, the baler compresses the loose hay along the bale's length when it is being made. Second, the twines run lengthwise. This means that the twine restrains the bale's spring back in the same direction as the hay was originally compressed. Finally, the loops of twine sit at different positions across the width of the bale.

\subsection{Different Tensions}
In hay manufacturing, tension can describe three separate things.
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Chamber tension: the tension set by a spring which squeezes the bale chamber. It changes bale density.
	\item Twine tensioner: %TODO twine tensioner explainer
	\item Tension in finished bale: the force carried by the twines in the finished bale.
\end{enumerate}
For this investigation, the one that matters most is the tension in a finished bale. As the bale is no longer in the chamber, it is not compressed and will expand until its residual outward pressure balances the force applied by the twine. This value results from multiple factors, including the other two types of tension, hay properties, and twine properties. It gives us the best perspective on a bale's shape and stiffness.
