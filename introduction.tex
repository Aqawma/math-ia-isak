\section{Introduction}

I have spent my entire life living on a hay farm, where we make small square bales. These bales are approximately 22 kilograms in mass and 91, 41, 36 centimeters in length, width, and height. These bales are tied with two lengths of twine by a baler. A photo of a bale is given in Figure \ref{im:haybale}.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\fbox{\includegraphics[scale=.2]{haybale.jpg}}
	\caption{A small square bale \parencite{StrawBales2024}.}
	\label{im:haybale}
\end{figure}

During production, these bales are thrown from the baler into a wagon pulled behind. The haybales, while quite substantial, have a slight amount of flex in the direction parallel to the strings on the bale. This means that as the wagon fills with bales, they can warp slightly, affecting how many bales can fit in a wagon. Obviously, if the string on the bale is tied tighter, then there will be less flex, so that fact led to my curiosity about whether the bale string tightness has any impact on the packing efficiency of a hay wagon.
